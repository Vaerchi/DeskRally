# DeskRally licensing plan

## Selected license

Apache License 2.0 is selected for original DeskRally product work. The unmodified license text is in `LICENSE`, with project attribution in `NOTICE`. The separately maintained DeskRally-Website repository carries its own project license and notices.

Apache 2.0 is a permissive license. It permits commercial and noncommercial use and modification subject to its conditions, and includes explicit contributor patent terms, a warranty disclaimer, and a limitation of liability. It does not require downstream users to publish their modifications. The actual license text controls.

The license does not guarantee immunity from liability. Its limitations are subject to applicable law and agreements; running a hosted service creates operational questions that a source-code license does not by itself resolve. Do not advertise the license as eliminating all responsibility or guaranteeing the software is safe.

## Application source migration

The application source is not yet included in this repository. Before importing it:

1. Confirm the project has the rights needed to license each original contribution.
2. Inventory Rust, Go, JavaScript, Android, native, vendored, and binary dependencies, including build-time code distributed with releases.
3. Retain third-party licenses, copyright notices, and required attribution. A project license does not relicense someone else's work.
4. Review dependency compatibility and any source-distribution or notice obligations before producing binaries.
5. Add appropriate license identifiers to original source files, preserving existing third-party headers.
6. Include `LICENSE`, `NOTICE`, and required dependency notices in source and binary release packages.

Do not remove legally required attribution in a privacy cleanup. Keep private account information, personal machine paths, operational notes, portraits, and secrets out of the release separately.

## Contributions and identity

Use the project/contributor designation for project attribution without publishing personal contact or location details. Keep records sufficient to establish who owns and may license original contributions. Do not invent a legal entity or claim ownership of third-party work.

Before accepting outside contributions, publish contribution guidance and a clear policy that contributions are submitted under the project license. Apache 2.0 does not by itself make a separate contributor agreement mandatory.

The license does not grant a general right to use project trade names or trademarks. Avoid adding custom use restrictions to the Apache license text; describe branding separately if needed.

## Website and launch

The site may describe Apache 2.0 as the chosen project license, while stating honestly that the public source release is still being prepared. The repository remains private during this work. No license choice changes repository or website access settings.

Before public release, review the complete source and distributions, required notices, reporting route, and ownership questions. A qualified attorney in the relevant jurisdiction can advise on the specific liability and hosting situation; this plan is not a legal opinion.
